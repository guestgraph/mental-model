# Agents write the model, and a person approves every change to it

> The pages of GuestGraph's model are written by agents, on a person's request or in a workflow that runs on its own, and no change enters the model until a person who answers for it has approved it; the checks prove a change has the right shape, and only the person approving it says it is true.

## The question

Who writes our model, and who lets a change into it. Agents already draft and implement under the seats our processes name, and CompanyGraph decided on September 29, 2026 that a company's model is written this way. It had to be decided for our model then too, because the model is what a hotel, a partner or an agent reads about GuestGraph.

## Alternatives

| Option | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| People write the pages by hand | The format costs a person time an agent spends without effort, and that time is better spent on whether a page is true. |
| A change from a workflow that runs on its own merges once the checks pass | A check proves a page has the right shape, not that it is true, and nobody would answer for what the model then claims about GuestGraph. |

## Why

What only a person can supply is whether a change is true and whether we mean it. Putting the writing with agents and the approving with a person spends a person's time on the one part nobody else can do.

## Consequences

Every change reaches the model through a person's approval, whether a person asked for it or a workflow made it, and nothing merges on the checks alone. Answers on the chat change nothing and still go out unread. The call is about our model, not the engine: whether an agent may decide a guest merge is the product's own question, and the objective on merges answers it. The call stays right for as long as the person approving actually judges each change.
